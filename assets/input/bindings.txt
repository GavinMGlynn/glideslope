# What each input of a flight controller does, by the kind of device SDL says
# it is: flight_stick (sticks and yokes, and anything SDL cannot name) or
# throttle (a HOTAS throttle or a quadrant).
#
#   KIND axis N CONTROL centred SCALE     -1..1 to the control, times SCALE
#   KIND axis N CONTROL lever SCALE       -1..1, times SCALE, to 0..1
#   KIND button N CONTROL hold            the control at 1 while held
#   KIND button N CONTROL step AMOUNT     the control moved by AMOUNT at each press
#   KIND hat N up|down|left|right CONTROL step AMOUNT
#
# CONTROL: aileron elevator rudder pitch_trim (-1..1), throttle mixture
# propeller flaps left_brake right_brake brakes (0..1). A stick pushed forward
# reads -1, so the elevator's scale is 1 (nose down) and a lever's is -1
# (forward is full). Flaps move in steps of a third: 10 degrees of the
# Cessna's 30.
#
# **A quadrant's three levers sit in the order the hand finds them**: throttle,
# propeller, mixture, on its first three axes. The rudder is not on the
# quadrant, because that is not where a rudder belongs. A propeller lever is
# the rpm lever of a constant-speed airscrew and the pitch lever of a
# two-pitch one - 1 is its highest rpm or fine pitch - and an aeroplane with
# neither ignores it.
#
# Every axis, button and hat direction a device of up to eight axes, sixteen
# buttons and one hat can have is bound; tests/unit/test_input.cpp walks them.
